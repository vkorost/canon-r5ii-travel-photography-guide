# Research, sourcing and labeling

A manual states what it states. Everything a guide adds comes from somewhere else, and **the value of the addition depends entirely on the reader being able to tell how firmly it is held**.

## The manual's silences drive the research

Before writing each chapter, write down **what the manual raises and does not answer**. Those lists drove the research for this book, and most of the genuinely useful findings came out of them.

This camera's manual never states the still-image bit depth, gives no stabilization figure in stops, quotes autofocus sensitivity for a lens two and a half stops faster than any zoom in the bag, and does not list which lenses carry correction data. **Every confident claim on the internet about those four things is outside the manual**, and you cannot tell that from reading it.

A manual never says what it does not say, and that invisibility is the single biggest reason a manual falls short of being a guide.

## Three grades, visible in the sentence

Not in a footnote. In the sentence, where a reader cannot skip past it.

1. **Documented.** The manual says it. Quoted, with a page number in Appendix C.
2. **Reported.** Owners say it. Named by source, with the number of independent reports where that is known, and phrased as a report: *one thread describes…*, *a contributor names…*
3. **Not found.** Written down as not found, rather than left out. Three questions in this book carry a sentence saying no source was located, which is more useful than silence and far more useful than a guess.

## Contradictions get recorded, not resolved

Where owners contradict the documentation, the book says so and attributes both sides. Two survive in the text: a report that one setting is silently overridden when another is enabled, and a report that one battery model causes the power-saving timers to be ignored. Both rest on one report each, and the text labels them that way.

**Where there is no consensus, say so** rather than picking a side. A surviving "no consensus" costs a reader a little certainty, where a manufactured one costs them a setting that is wrong on their camera.

## Research agents come back confident either way

An agent reported that a tracking setting had three states. The manual gives two. It was caught by checking the page before writing. Had it not been, three separate mode recipes in this book would have rested on a setting that does not exist in that form.

Brief them to name sources inline, to distinguish your hardware from adjacent models, and to write **not found** rather than inferring. Then verify anything load-bearing against the primary document yourself. The register of a confident wrong answer and a confident right one is identical.

## Compute rather than cite, where you can

Every number the book can derive, it derives, and it shows the formula so a reader with different hardware can redo it. A computed number is checkable; a cited one is only as good as its source, and on this kind of subject the sources disagree. One lens's corner falloff was measured at 2.1 stops by one lab and about 0.4 by two others. The book says exactly that, and tells the reader to trust their own files over anyone's numbers.
